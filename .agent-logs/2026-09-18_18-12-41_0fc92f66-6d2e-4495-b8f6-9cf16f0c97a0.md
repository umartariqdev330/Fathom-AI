Design pass done and committed. Here's what I found and changed.

## The four things actually holding it back

**1. The player read as a placeholder, not a product.** A dark box with an avatar and centred text floating in it. Rebuilt as a real recording surface: platform chip with a live indicator top-left, participant stack top-right, speaker centred, and the current line as a **live caption bar** with a gradient at the bottom — where captions actually belong. The control row was one crowded strip of eight controls; it's now a scrubber row (elapsed · track · duration) above a transport row with left controls and right-grouped utilities.

**2. The transcript repeated speaker identity on every line.** Real transcripts group a speaker's consecutive lines into one turn, and 234 segments of avatar-name-avatar-name was noisy. Consecutive lines now group under a single name. The active line uses a left accent rule instead of a full background tint — less shouty, easier to track while playing.

**3. Text ran to 140-character lines.** Meeting summaries stretched the full width of a 1536px screen. Capped at 78ch, with the summary panel at `max-w-3xl` and line-height raised to 1.65.

## The one-line version

It's a note-taker for meetings. You record a conversation, and it gives you back a written record, a summary, and a to-do list — so nobody has to sit there typing notes.

## What actually happens when you use it

1. **You hit Record.** Your browser asks to use your microphone. It captures whoever is in the room with you.
2. **You hit Stop.** The audio gets sent to your server and saved as a file.
3. **It writes down what was said** — the full conversation, with timestamps.
4. **It reads that back and summarises it** — the main points, what got decided, what the topics were.
5. **It pulls out the to-dos** — "Sarah is sending the contract by Friday" becomes an actual task with a name and a date.
6. **You can listen back**, and the written text scrolls along with the audio. Click any line and the audio jumps to that moment.

## The four things that make it useful afterwards

**Search across everything.** You remember someone mentioned a price problem but not which meeting. Type "pricing" and it finds every time anyone said it, in any meeting, and takes you to that exact second.

**Star the good bits.** Something important gets said — click the star. It saves that moment with the words attached, and you can jump straight back to it later.

**Send someone a 30-second piece.** Pick a start and end, and it cuts that chunk out of the recording into its own file and gives you a link. Anyone can open it — no account, no login. That's for when your colleague missed the call and you don't want to make them sit through the whole hour.

**Read it a different way.** The same meeting can be summarised as a sales call (what the customer needs, what they objected to) or as an engineering review (what was decided, what's blocking) — and the content genuinely changes, not just the headings.

It comes pre-loaded with eight made-up-but-realistic meetings, so anyone opening it sees a working product instead of an empty screen.
